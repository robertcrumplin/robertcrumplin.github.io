Genus one mirror symmetry and surfaces with trivial first Chern class

Gabriel Ab\'anades-Joglar, Dennis Eriksson

arXiv:2611.34594 · pdf · math.AG math-ph math.MP · score 13

The main goal of this paper is to study the extension of genus-one BCOV type mirror symmetry for smooth projective varieties with vanishing $c_1(X)$. We show that the naive generalization of the expectation that one can encode genus one Gromov-Witten invariants in the BCOV invariant of the mirror family cannot hold in this setup. We show this by completing the study of these invariants for surfaces.

On the unirationality of anticanonical weighted complete intersection surfaces: the infinite families

Giulia Gugiatti, Alex Massarenti · Giulia Gugiatti is a student of Corti and Coates

arXiv:2611.31552 · pdf · math.AG math.NT · score 7

Anticanonical weighted complete intersection surfaces fall into finitely many sporadic families and four infinite families. We prove that the anticanonical surfaces lying in the infinite families are unirational over their field of definition. Over a Hilbertian field, we further prove that a Hilbert subset of members consists of nonrational surfaces.

Theta Functions and Intrinsic Mirrors of Laurent Phenomenon Algebras

Jiarui Fei

arXiv:2611.32571 · pdf · math.AG math.AC math.CO · score 17

We construct bases of upper Laurent phenomenon algebras over a field by minimizing Newton polytopes in a single seed. For any translation-invariant total order on the exponents, each least exponent $a$ that occurs determines a smallest Newton polytope $Q_a$. Choose one element with Newton polytope $Q_a$ and least term $x^a$ for each such $a$. These elements form a basis.

Virasoro operators for relative Gromov–Witten theory

Shuai Guo, Longting Wu, Lei You

arXiv:2611.30566 · pdf · math.AG math-ph math.MP · score 18

Let $D$ be a smooth divisor in a smooth projective variety $X$. We construct relative Virasoro operators acting on the relative descendant potential of $(X,D)$. Each operator is the logarithmic Virasoro operator of $(X,D)$ plus a boundary correction determined only by $D$ and its normal bundle in $X$.

Rank-Two Attractors and Modularity for a Mirror Bicubic

Yibing Chen, Emanuel Scheidegger

arXiv:2611.30234 · pdf · math.AG hep-th math.NT · score 5

We study the mirror family $\mathcal X_\lambda$ of the bicubic Calabi-Yau threefold, obtained from a family of complete intersections of two cubics in $\mathbb P^5$. We show that the four-dimensional weight-three Hodge structure of the fibers over $\lambda^6 = -8$ splits into two rational rank-two pieces of Hodge types $(1,0,0,1)$ and $(0,1,1,0)$; in particular, these points are rank-two attractor points.

A Determinant Proof of the Sun–Yang–Zuo Conjecture

Jinbang Yang

arXiv:2611.29308 · pdf · math.AG math.AC math.RA · score 4

We give an elementary proof of the determinant identity in \cite[Conjecture~A.1]{SYZ} over any field of odd characteristic $p$. The proof computes the determinant of a single linear map by dividing by two different monic polynomials. By \cite[Proposition~A.9]{SYZ}, the identity gives another proof of the Sun–Yang–Zuo conjecture.

Combinatorics of equivariant blowups of spherical varieties

Daniil Shunin

arXiv:2611.29490 · pdf · math.AG · score 4

We explicitly describe the colored fan of the blowup of a smooth spherical variety $X$ along a smooth irreducible $G$-stable subvariety $Y$. The answer is expressed in terms of the colored cone of $Y$ and the localization of the valuation cone at $Y$, and generalizes the star subdivision of fans of toric varieties. We also consider several examples illustrating the main result.

Combinatorial relative orientability for toric and tropical geometry

Nathan Tiggemann

arXiv:2611.27238 · pdf · math.AG · score 17

Many problems in enumerative geometry are solved by computing the degree of the top Chern class of a suitable vector bundle. Results from $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy theory allow to answer enumerative questions over arbitrary fields, replacing the top Chern class by the Euler class in Chow-Witt groups. For its degree to be well-defined, one needs the vector bundle to be relatively oriented.
